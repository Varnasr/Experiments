How the days are counted, and what this page leaves out

The day the authority received the application is day zero and the count starts on the next day, so an application received on 1 March has its thirtieth day on 31 March. The same rule is used for every period on this page. It is the ordinary rule for a period that runs "from" a day, and it is stated here as the page's assumption.

A last day that falls on a Saturday or a Sunday is marked. The page does not know gazetted holidays and does not move a date. Where an office is closed on the last day, the act can ordinarily be done on the next day the office is open, and the safe course is to file before the marked date.

Three readings are the page's own. First, where the application went to an Assistant Public Information Officer and concerns life or liberty, the five days are added to the forty-eight hours, because the proviso to section 5(2) adds them to "the period for response specified under sub-section (1) of section 7" and the forty-eight hours sit in that sub-section. Second, with a third-party notice the period is forty days and nothing is added or left out, because section 11(3) sets that period "notwithstanding anything contained in section 7". Third, where the first appellate authority has not decided, the ninety days for the second appeal are counted from the thirtieth day after the first appeal was filed. Section 19(6) allows up to forty-five days with written reasons, so the later date is shown beside the earlier one, and the earlier one is the date to file by.

On a transfer the Act says the transfer must be made within five days and does not say in terms when the second authority's thirty days begin. The page counts from the day the second authority received the application when you enter it, and shows beside it the date reached by allowing five days for the transfer and then the period, from the first receipt. The two differ by the time the application spent in transit.

The page covers the central Act's time limits only: sections 5(2), 6(3), 7, 11 and 19. It does not cover fees, the exemptions in sections 8 and 9, the form of an appeal, or the rules of any State or court. An appeal filed late can still be admitted for sufficient cause under the provisos to section 19(1) and 19(3), and the page does not judge that. The first appeal is taken as received on the day it was filed.
