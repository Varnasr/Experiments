Econometrics and measurement, computed entirely in the page. Each one checks itself against an independent reference and says so.

Causal Inference WorkbenchDataResearch

Difference-in-differences, event studies and synthetic control on any panel: simulate one with a known true effect, upload a CSV, or pull a live country panel from the World Bank and name the treated country.

HTML · JavaScript · World Bank API · no dependencies

In plain words. When a policy reaches some places and not others, the question is what would have happened without it. These three methods each build a counterfactual from the untreated units and measure the gap. The simulator plants a known effect so you can watch each estimator recover it, or fail to when parallel trends are violated.
  • DiD. Two-way fixed effects removed by alternating demeaning (so unbalanced panels work), then OLS on the residualised variables by Cholesky. Standard errors are the cluster-robust sandwich with the fixest small-sample convention (nested fixed effects not counted in k); the CI uses a t with G−1 degrees of freedom.
  • Event study. Relative-time indicators with binned endpoints and k = −1 omitted; a Wald test on the pre-period coefficients. The Sun–Abraham (2021) option interacts cohort with relative time against never-treated units and averages with cohort-share weights, standard errors by the delta method on the full covariance.
  • Synthetic control. Weights on the simplex found by accelerated projected gradient with the exact simplex projection of Duchi et al. (2008); placebo-in-space inference ranks the post/pre RMSPE ratio against every donor treated in turn. The page warns when donors outnumber pre-periods, the over-fitting case Abadie (2021) describes.
  • Charts are hand-rolled SVG with hover tooltips; no charting library.
Verified: the TWFE estimate and clustered SE match an explicit-dummies regression written separately in Python to six and four decimals.

Poverty & Inequality LabDataResearch

What POVCAL did with grouped data: Lorenz curves fitted to decile shares pulled live from the World Bank's Poverty and Inequality Platform, then Gini, Theil, Atkinson, Palma and FGT poverty at any line, the Datt–Ravallion decomposition and a growth incidence curve.

HTML · JavaScript · PIP API · no dependencies

In plain words. Published inequality data usually comes as ten numbers, the share of income going to each tenth of the population, not as a household file. From those ten numbers and the average, this page reconstructs the whole distribution and reads off how many people fall below any poverty line, how unequal the country is, and whether growth between two surveys reached the poor. One click compares its answers with the World Bank's own, computed from the microdata.
  • Lorenz curves after Datt (1998): the General Quadratic (Villasenor and Arnold 1989) and Beta (Kakwani 1980) forms, each fitted by OLS to the cumulative decile points. Validity is checked on a 2,000-point grid (0 ≤ L ≤ p, L′ non-negative and non-decreasing) and against the theoretical conditions; the valid curve with the smaller squared error wins.
  • Measures come from the fitted quantile function y(p) = μ·L′(p) on a 4,000-point grid: Gini, Theil T, mean log deviation, Atkinson at three aversions, Palma, P90/P10, median; the headcount by bisection, FGT1 and FGT2 by integration to the headcount, Watts.
  • Change over time: Datt–Ravallion (1992) growth and redistribution with its residual, the Shapley version without one, and the Ravallion–Chen (2003) growth incidence curve.
  • Data from api.worldbank.org/pip/v1 (2021 PPP), country list from the PIP auxiliary tables; the CSP on this site allows only that host.
Verified: on India 2022 the page gives Gini 0.256 and a $3.00 headcount of 5.1% against PIP's microdata 0.255 and 5.3%; the GQ results match Datt's closed-form formulas to four decimals.

Survey Data AuditorDataTool

The checks a monitoring team runs before anyone estimates anything: duplicate and near-duplicate interviews, Benford's law on amounts, age heaping, straightlining, interview length and timing, missingness, and an enumerator profile that says who to call back.

HTML · JavaScript · no dependencies

In plain words. Field surveys go wrong in predictable ways: an interviewer copies yesterday's form under a new number, invents incomes, rounds every age to a five, answers a whole block of questions with the same tick, or finishes a forty-minute interview in eight. Drop in the CSV and the page finds each pattern, names the enumerator, and writes the call-back list. Nothing is uploaded anywhere.
  • Duplicates: exact matches by hashing every column except ID and timestamps; near-duplicates by pairwise comparison within an enumerator, flagged at 90% identical fields (sampled above 4,000 interviews).
  • Benford: first-digit distribution against log10(1 + 1/d), judged by Nigrini's mean-absolute-deviation cut-offs rather than χ², which over-rejects in large samples; per-enumerator verdicts use the χ² p-value because MAD is unreliable below a few hundred values. Only offered for columns whose 5th to 95th percentile spans at least tenfold.
  • Age heaping: Whipple's index on ages 23 to 62 with the UN scale, Myers' blended index on ages 10 to 89; per-enumerator flags require the 0/5 share to be significantly above 20%.
  • Straightlining against a chance benchmark of m1−k; duration under 40% of the median; night starts; rounding of second decimals (true zeros excluded); outliers by MAD-based z.
  • Who to call: every metric z-scored across enumerators, two flags puts a name on the list. Exports Markdown and CSV.
Verified: the seeded demo plants six problems across five enumerators and the audit recovers all six, attributed to the right people; Whipple, Myers and the Benford expectation were checked by hand.

Motor Accident Compensation CalculatorLawTool

Fatal-accident awards under the Motor Vehicles Act, step by step with the authority for each line: the Sarla Verma multiplier, Pranay Sethi future prospects and conventional heads with their 10% triennial enhancement, consortium per dependant, and interest.

HTML · JavaScript · no dependencies

In plain words. When someone dies in a road accident, Indian tribunals value the income the family has lost using rules the Supreme Court has fixed: how much of the income the deceased would have spent on themself, how much it would have grown, and how many years to count. Enter the age, income and family, and the page produces the award the way a tribunal would write it, with the case and paragraph next to every figure. Where the judgments disagree, it offers both readings.
  • Rules encoded: Sarla Verma (2009) 6 SCC 121 paras 30 to 32 and 42 (deductions, multiplier table); Pranay Sethi (2017) 16 SCC 680 para 61 (future prospects by employment and age, conventional heads, enhancement every three years from 31 October 2017); Magma (2018), Somwati (2020) and Sameem Begum (2026) on consortium to each dependant; section 171 interest.
  • Enhancement is computed as 1.1⌊months/36⌋ from the judgment date to the award date, which reproduces the ₹16,500 / ₹44,000 and ₹18,150 / ₹48,400 figures the courts have applied since.
  • Unsettled points are surfaced, not hidden: consortium once or per dependant, enhancement by award or accident date, a married deceased with one dependant, ages above the table.
  • Print stylesheet produces a clean award sheet.
Verified: reproduces a published worked example (age 38, ₹4,004 a month, eight dependants: loss of dependency ₹8,64,864) and the ₹48,400 consortium the Supreme Court applied in August 2026.

Court Petition TranslatorAITool

Translates Hindi Supreme Court and High Court petitions into formal legal English. Handles the legalese, so yaachikaakarta becomes "Petitioner" and anuchhed becomes "Article", and the output reads like it was drafted in English.

Sarvam AI · Llama 3.3 70B via Groq · Netlify Functions

In plain words. Paste the Hindi text of a petition and get back English that a lawyer would recognise as a petition, with the right terms of art, rather than a literal translation.
  • Text is chunked and sent to sarvam-translate:v1 in formal mode, then the draft is revised by Llama 3.3 70B on Groq with a prompt that fixes legal terminology (याचिकाकर्ता → Petitioner, माननीय → Hon'ble, अनुच्छेद → Article).
  • Keys stay server-side in a Netlify Function (netlify/functions/court-translate.js); the browser only ever talks to that function.
  • Stage: prototype. The evaluation that is missing is a blind comparison against a human legal translator on a sample of real petitions.

Drive PDF ExtractorTool

Headless-browser command line for view-only Google Drive PDFs: scrolls the Drive viewer, captures each rendered page, and reassembles them into a fresh PDF. No Drive authentication needed.

Node · Playwright · jsPDF

In plain words. Some documents shared on Google Drive can be read but not downloaded. This script reads them the way you would, page by page, and saves what it sees as a normal PDF you can keep and search.
  • Playwright drives Chromium through the Drive viewer, scrolling until every page canvas has rendered, then exports each canvas as an image and stitches them with jsPDF at the original page dimensions.
  • Runs locally from the terminal; nothing is uploaded and no credentials are stored. Internal tool, used for research archives.

Kundendu's Learn & PlayFamilyCreative

A Class 1 CBSE worksheet generator with a structured course beside it: English phonics and reading, Hindi, Maths and EVS. Fresh questions every time, three difficulty levels, on-screen marking with confetti, printable sheets with answer keys, and eight decodable readers with read-along audio.

HTML · CSS · JavaScript · PWA · no dependencies

Also at littlek.in

In plain words. A six-year-old's practice app. It never shows the same worksheet twice, it checks answers on screen, and its course introduces sounds before letters in the order that makes readable words fastest. Every lesson has a note for the grown-up on how to teach the rule.
  • 36 activity types generated from rules, not stored questions, seeded per sheet so a printed answer key matches its worksheet.
  • The course is a directed sequence of 161 lessons across four paths (synthetic-phonics scope and sequence, number sense, Hindi varnamala and matras, EVS), each unlocking the next at 80%.
  • Decodable readers use only the sounds taught so far; read-along audio is word-timed. Word and Google Docs export for the printable sheets.
  • Installable PWA with a small service worker, so it works offline on a tablet after the first visit; progress is stored locally on the device.

HTML · CSS · JavaScript · PWA · no dependencies

Also at alab.cloud

Notes on the stack

How these are made, and why this way

One file, no build. Each tool is a single HTML file with its CSS and JavaScript inline, deployed as-is by Netlify on push. There is no bundler, no framework and no package to install, which means a tool from this page still opens in ten years, can be read in full by anyone who views source, and can be forked by copying one file. The two AI tools are the exception: their keys live in Netlify Functions so the browser never holds a secret.

Compute in the browser. The econometrics and the poverty measures are implemented from the papers rather than imported: Cholesky solves, alternating-projection demeaning, cluster-robust sandwiches, simplex projection, Lorenz-curve fitting, numerical integration and bisection are all written in plain JavaScript in the page. Survey data and panels never leave the machine. The only network calls are to two public World Bank APIs, and the site's Content-Security-Policy allows nothing else.

Every number is checked against something outside the page.

  • TWFE coefficient and clustered standard error against an explicit-dummies regression written independently in Python.
  • General Quadratic poverty measures against Datt's closed-form formulas; Gini and headcounts against PIP's own microdata figures.
  • Whipple, Myers and Benford against hand-computed limiting cases; the demo audit against the problems planted in it.
  • The compensation calculator against a published worked example and against figures the Supreme Court applied in 2026.

Tested in a real browser. Every page is driven in headless Chromium before it is pushed: the estimators are run on simulated and recorded data, the console is required to be empty, and screenshots are checked at desktop and phone widths. Charts are drawn as SVG by hand with hover tooltips, one axis, and a palette validated for colour-vision deficiency.

Written to be read. Every tool carries a "what is being estimated" or "method" note beside its output, its README states what was verified and how, and the places where a method or a judgment is unsettled are said out loud rather than smoothed over.
