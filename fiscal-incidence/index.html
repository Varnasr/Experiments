Lorenz and concentration curves

Method

Groups are ranked by income before taxes and transfers. The Gini coefficient is one minus twice the area under the Lorenz curve drawn through the group points. A concentration coefficient is the same calculation for a tax or a transfer with the groups still ranked by that income (Kakwani 1977). A tax is progressive when its concentration coefficient is above the Gini, so the Kakwani index is CT − GX. For a transfer the index is GX − CB, the convention of the Commitment to Equity handbook (Lustig 2018), so that a positive number means progressive for both. A transfer with a negative concentration coefficient gives more rupees per person to poorer groups.

The redistributive effect is the Gini before minus the Gini after (Reynolds and Smolensky 1977). It splits into vertical equity, GX minus the concentration coefficient of final income ranked by initial income, less reranking, the Gini of final income minus that same coefficient (Atkinson 1980; Plotnick 1981). Vertical equity also equals (t·KT + b·KB)/(1 − t + b), where t and b are taxes and transfers as shares of initial income (Lambert 2001), and the page prints both so the identity can be seen to hold. The marginal contribution of one instrument is the Gini of final income without it, minus the Gini of final income with it.
