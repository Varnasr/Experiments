Multidimensional Poverty Alkire–Foster headcount · intensity · MPI

ExperimentsSource

Poverty counted across several deprivations at once. Give the page a file with one row per person and a 0 or 1 for each deprivation, choose the weights and the cutoff, and it returns how many people are poor, how deprived they are on average, which deprivations account for the index, and which groups carry it.

Data

The example is simulated: 2,400 people in three regions with the ten indicators of the global index. It describes no country. The file is read in this page and is not uploaded.

Indicators

A 1 means deprived. Weights are rescaled to add to one, so 1, 1, 2 is read as a quarter, a quarter and a half.

Method

Each person's deprivation score is the weighted sum of the indicators in which they are deprived. A person is poor when the score is at or above the cutoff k. The headcount H is the share of people who are poor, the intensity A is the average score among the poor, and the index is M₀ = H × A, the adjusted headcount ratio of Alkire and Foster (2011). The censored headcount of an indicator is the share of people who are poor and deprived in it; weighted and summed over indicators it gives M₀ again, so each indicator's contribution is its weight times its censored headcount over M₀. For groups, M₀ is the population-weighted average of the group values. Vulnerable means a score of at least 0.2 and below the cutoff; severe means a score of at least 0.5, the thresholds the global index uses with a cutoff of one third (Alkire, Kanagaratnam and Suppa, OPHI).
