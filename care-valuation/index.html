A rupee value for the unpaid domestic and care work that women and men do in each Indian state. The minutes come from the Time Use Survey and the wages from the Periodic Labour Force Survey, for 2024 or for 2019, and the page sets the two years side by side in 2024 prices. Every assumption that turns a wage into an hourly rate is an input you can change.

Method and limits

Minutes are the average per person per day, counting people who did none of the activity as zero. When a person reported two or three activities in the same time slot, the slot is shared equally among them, which is how the survey's own published tables count time "irrespective of major activity". Domestic services are ICATUS major division 3 and caregiving is major division 4. The state figures were computed from the unit-level data with the survey multipliers, and the same computation returns the published all-India figures: 283 and 58 minutes for women aged 15 to 59, 26 and 16 for men.

The value is a replacement cost at a generalist wage: what the hours would cost if someone were paid the state's average wage to do them. It is a floor for some tasks and a ceiling for others. Childcare and care of the sick are valued at the same wage as sweeping. Supervision that overlaps another task gets only its share of the slot.

Valuing each sex at its own wage builds the gender wage gap into the answer: an hour of a woman's cooking is then worth less than an hour of a man's. The default prices both at the wage of all workers in the state.

Small states and union territories rest on few sample households. The standard error beside each minutes figure treats the first-stage sample unit as a cluster and ignores stratification. A wage that rests on fewer than 30 workers in the survey sample is not used. The published casual wage for women is ₹200 in Chandigarh and ₹500 in Mizoram, and each comes from one woman; Delhi's ₹500 comes from three. Eighteen casual-wage cells fall below 30 workers, most of them women in small states and union territories, and two regular-wage cells do. Lakshadweep has no woman in casual labour in the sample. The page gives a value per person and no state total, because a total needs the population in each age group.

The 2019 figures come from different files. MoSPI's unit-level file for the 2019 survey has no start or end time on any diary row, so the minutes are the ones printed in the survey report, Statement 7.1, in whole minutes, with the relative standard errors of its Table (3RSE). Those errors are the survey office's own and are larger than the ones computed here for 2024, which ignore stratification: for women aged 15 to 59 across India the 2019 error is 2.3 minutes on 290 and the 2024 error is 0.8 on 283. The 2019 wages are computed from the unit-level data of two survey rounds, January to June 2019 from the 2018-19 round and July to December 2019 from the 2019-20 round, because the published figures are by quarter. The computation returns MoSPI's all-India quarterly regular wages to 0.15% and casual wages to 1.75%. The 2019 cells count every visit to an urban household and the 2024 cells count the first visit only, so the worker counts of the two years are not comparable, and a state's regular wage can move with who is in the sample.

Prices: 2019 rupees are multiplied by the ratio of the all-India Consumer Price Index averaged over 2024 to its average over 2019, base 2012, which is . One national index is used for every state. In 2019 Dadra and Nagar Haveli and Daman and Diu were separate and Ladakh was part of Jammu and Kashmir, so those places have no comparison.

Hours are what the same workers reported for the survey week: for casual labourers the hours on a day of casual work, and for regular employees the hours in all their work that week. Across India in 2024 a casual day is 7.7 hours and a regular employee's week is 48.8 hours.

Sources: Time Use Survey 2024, Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation, unit-level data. Periodic Labour Force Survey, calendar year 2024: wages from indicators 6 and 7 through the MoSPI data service, read on 11 November 2026; sample counts and hours from the unit-level data. For 2019: Time Use in India-2019, National Statistical Office, 2020; Periodic Labour Force Survey unit-level data, July 2018 to June 2019 and July 2019 to June 2020; Consumer Price Index, base 2012, MoSPI.
